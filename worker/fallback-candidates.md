## 1. Edmond Edward Wysinger (1816-1891)

- Gold Rush pioneer: arrived in California around October 1849; mined at
  Mokelumne, Murphy's Camp, Grass Valley, Negro Bar; bought his own freedom
  for $1,000 in about a year.
- Moved to Visalia around 1862; self-educated laborer and part-time preacher.
- October 1888: tried to enroll his son Arthur in the regular Visalia public
  school, was refused and sent to the colored school; filed suit (writ of
  mandate) against the county school board.
- Wysinger v. Crookshank, 82 Cal 588 (1890): decided January 29, 1890; the
  California Supreme Court reversed the lower court and ordered 12-year-old
  Arthur admitted to Visalia's regular school system.
- Why it matters: a California court struck down school segregation for Black
  children 64 years before Brown v. Board. In April 2025, Visalia Unified
  named the Redwood High academic building for him after a student-led proposal.

## 2. Sarah Massey Overton (c. 1850 - August 24, 1914)

- Born in Massachusetts around 1850; moved with family to Gilroy, then San Jose,
  California as a young girl; attended the Phoenixonian Institute (founded 1861,
  California's first Black secondary school).
- 1869: married Jacob Overton; the couple ran a catering business in San Jose.
- 1880s: leader in the fight for Black children to attend California public
  schools; traveled California and Nevada drumming up support.
- 1906: co-founded San Jose's Garden City Women's Club; lobbied for interracial
  women's club coalitions for women's suffrage; vice-president of San Jose's
  interracial Suffrage Amendment League ahead of the 1911 statewide vote.
- President of the all-Black Victoria Earle Matthews (Mothers) Club, which aided
  young Black women and girls facing or threatened with sexual abuse.
- Why it matters: she bridged the fights for Black education and women's
  suffrage in California, building interracial coalitions when few did.
